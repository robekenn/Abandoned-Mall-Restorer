# Two sides of every court

All four sections now contain ten businesses: five along the original row and five opposite it. Courts are deeper to keep the fountain, benches, deliveries and walking routes clear. Lower-row doors face into the court; shoppers enter and exit through that side. Their owners offer the same three introductory projects and twelve rotating favors. All ten businesses, plus the first cleanup, must be completed before the next area gate appears.

Initial litter pools contain 63 North, 56 East, 51 Garden and 54 Commons pieces. Every floor tile remains covered by reachable cleanup, including beneath props. Equipment, fixture prices, rent cleanliness tiers and the four-second recurring litter timer retain their existing rules.

## Hire local help

Open J and select **Janitors**. One janitor may be hired per unlocked section. The price is exactly twice that section's most expensive business. No repeat purchase or additional janitor is allowed. The journal shows current speed, cleaning time, pieces cleaned and money earned.

| Janitor | Most expensive business | Hiring cost | Upgrade prices per track |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| North | $3,200 | $6,400 | $1,600 / $3,200 / $6,400 |
| East | $25,000 | $50,000 | $12,500 / $25,000 / $50,000 |
| Garden | $66,000 | $132,000 | $33,000 / $66,000 / $132,000 |
| Commons | $173,000 | $346,000 | $86,500 / $173,000 / $346,000 |

Each janitor starts at **48 pixels/second** and takes **5 seconds** to clean one piece of trash or dirt. They walk around obstacles to litter in their own court, remain beside it while working, then automatically sell that single item for **75% of the player's current per-item value**. Fractional dollars are retained: a $1 contract earns $0.75; $30 earns $22.50. Income goes straight to cash without occupying the player's bag or visiting a bin. Janitor income is independent of the rent cleanliness multiplier.

Cleaning upgrades reduce pickup time to **4, 3, then 2 seconds**. Walking upgrades increase speed to **64, 80, then 96 pixels/second**, still slower than the player's starting 240 pixels/second. The tracks are independent for each janitor and cost one quarter, one half, then the full hiring price. Maximum and unaffordable purchases never deduct money.

Janitors wander locally when no litter is present. If the player picks up a janitor's target, the janitor stops that work and looks for another piece; no duplicate sale is possible. Janitor cleanup restores floor cleanliness and can complete a first sweep, but does not count toward the player's tutorial or personal collection/sale favors. Gameplay menus pause janitor walking and work, including the journal itself. Close the journal to see a new hire start working.

Use **1/2/3** or **Tab** for Overview/Janitors/Story. In the Janitors tab, **Up/Down** selects a court, **Enter/Space** hires, **C** upgrades cleaning and **W** upgrades walking. Mouse controls work for every button. **J/Esc/E** returns to the mall. Progress, including janitors and unfinished cleaning work, saves on exit and resumes with Continue. Helpers leave the litter needed for your active collection favor; see [saving and story](saving-and-lantern-story.md).

## Review

Approach the lower storefronts at 800×600 and check their names and inward doors. Reopen both rows before opening the next section. Hire North's janitor, watch the slow walk and five-second progress bar, then compare a $0.75 automatic payout with the player's $1 sale. Collect a target mid-cleaning to check cancellation. Upgrade both tracks, hire in the later sections, and verify each cleaner remains local. Open the journal while cleaning to confirm the timer pauses. Automated checks cover every litter route, court boundaries, timed work, target cancellation and respawn, current-contract payouts, hiring prices, caps, independent upgrades, keyboard/mouse controls and forty-business progression.
